Research

Understanding the regenerative world around us.

Developing regenerative places and uplifting businesses.

Our natural environment has the energy to regenerate, evolve, and adapt. We can observe the patterns in nature that have these characteristics, and design them into our built environment.

Environmental Design / Urban Design Studio

Our urban design studio observes the energy nature uses to restore balance and bring new life and sustenance to eroded ground — and applies that same intelligence to the built environment. We start with the site conditions most visible in everyday practice, then move into the deeper ecological and sensory factors conventional design tends to overlook — mapping each back to the natural mechanisms and biophilic strategies that can bring it into balance.

Business Development Studio

Our business development studio applies that same systems-thinking approach to organizations — working with leadership to advance the vision; map how the business works to optimize sustainability, stability, and profitability; clarify the most meaningful metrics providing returns; and build upon the existing internal capacity, from team structure to succession planning, to effectively bring the business to new heights.

Essential sources

Selected readings that have inspired our work

  • FoundationalThinking in Systems: A Primer Donella H. Meadows (2008)

    A primer on systems theory, arguing that understanding behavior over time reveals more than isolated events ever could.

  • FoundationalGood to Great Jim Collins (2001)

    Distills five years of research into what separates companies that make the leap from good to great from those that don't.

  • FoundationalThe Fifth Discipline Peter M. Senge (1990)

    Names systems thinking as the discipline that ties together personal mastery, shared vision, mental models, and team learning in organizations.

  • Age of Entanglement Neri Oxman (2016)

    Traces design's shifting relationship to science, from the Enlightenment's neat disciplinary boundaries to today's entangled fields.

  • So Good They Can't Ignore You Cal Newport (2012)

    Argues mastery comes from accumulating rare, valuable skills — career capital — rather than chasing passion first.

  • Running Lean Ash Maurya (2010)

    A practitioner's playbook for startups, built on the idea that practice trumps theory when testing a new business model.

  • Mastery Robert Greene (2012)

    Makes the case that deep, undistracted focus — not talent alone — is what wires true skill into the brain.

  • Storytelling That Moves People Harvard Business Review (2003)

    On why persuasion through story, not data alone, is what actually moves people to act in business.
